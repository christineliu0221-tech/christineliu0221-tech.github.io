CASE STUDY 01 · ASSURANCE LEADERSHIP

SGX Listing Audit Workstream

Leading a complex cost-audit workstream required more than accurate testing. It required a clear method, coordinated execution, and conclusions that engagement leadership could review and use.

Environment
Listing-related audit
Team coordination
2 peer-level staff
Core judgment
Estimate-to-actual testing

CASE STRUCTURE

ContextContributionApproachOutcomeRelevance

01 · CONTEXT

The business and assurance question

A content-creator cost stream involved management estimates, individual agreements, accounting records, and subsequent actual amounts. The engagement needed a defensible view of whether recorded accruals were reasonable in a high-visibility listing environment.

02 · MY CONTRIBUTION

Clear ownership within a larger engagement

I served as the primary engagement staff for the workstream, coordinated two peer-level staff, and worked directly with the Senior Manager on the audit approach. My role covered both hands-on testing and the structure needed to keep the work consistent and reviewable.

03 · APPROACH

A structured path from evidence to conclusion

  1. 01

    Clarified the population, relevant agreements, accounting records, and estimate mechanics before testing.

  2. 02

    Selected and traced samples from streamer agreements to the underlying accounting records.

  3. 03

    Reconciled estimated cost accruals to subsequent actual amounts and investigated meaningful variances.

  4. 04

    Synthesized evidence and documented conclusions about the reasonableness of management's estimates.

04 · OUTCOME

Decision value

Delivered a reviewable body of work that supported engagement leadership's assessment of management's cost estimates while keeping testing, variance analysis, and conclusions connected.

05 · MARKET RELEVANCE

Why this work transfers

Audit and risk analytics roles still depend on the same core disciplines: define the question, understand the data-generating process, investigate exceptions, and communicate a conclusion that another professional can review. This case demonstrates judgment, ownership, and execution under pressure.
